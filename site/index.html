How it works

From a pencil to the deep sea

There are 12 creatures to colour. The tank knows every one of their outlines, so it can find your drawing, turn it the right way up and teach it to swim.

🖍️

Colour

Print a template and grab some pencils, or paint straight on your phone. Stay inside the lines or don't: it's your fish.

📷

Send

Snap a photo of your sheet, or tap “Swim!” on your phone. The tank cuts out your creature and recognises which of the 12 it is.

🐟

Swim

Each species moves its own way: jellyfish pulse up and down, sharks cruise and strike, the octopus ripples its arms, crabs stay on the sand.

The bubbles are real

The water is alive with Bitcoin

Every bubble rising from the sand is a real Bitcoin transaction waiting to be confirmed, live from the mempool. When a new block is found, the water flashes gold and every fish startles.

Bigger bubble = more bitcoin moved
Colour = the fee it pays, from patient to in a hurry
✨ Gold flash = a new block, about every 10 minutes
Where it started

It began at a party

In June 2026 we put a colouring table in a corner of a family party and a projector on the wall. Guests of every age coloured paper fish, a scanner sent them to the tank, and by the end of the night 46 creatures were swimming together.

Open source

Throw your own Fish Party

Birthdays, weddings, a classroom, a museum corner: the whole thing is free and runs on one laptop. Guests use their phones on your Wi-Fi; nothing needs the internet except the Bitcoin bubbles.

  • 💻A computer to run the tank (any laptop, or a Raspberry Pi 5).
  • 📽️A projector or a TV with a browser, pointed at the tank.
  • 📱Your guests' phones to paint, or to photograph their sheets.
  • 🖨️A printer and pencils if you want the paper version (kids love it).
# get it
git clone https://github.com/Rogzy-DB/fish-party
cd fish-party
python3 -m venv .venv && .venv/bin/pip install -r requirements.txt

# run the tank on your network
.venv/bin/python tank/serve.py --host 0.0.0.0 --port 8000

# projector → http://<your-computer>:8000/
# guests    → http://<your-computer>:8000/draw
